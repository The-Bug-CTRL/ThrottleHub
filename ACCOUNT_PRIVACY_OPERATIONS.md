# Account export/deletion operator workflow

This is an operator procedure for the designated private test cohort. It does not claim self-service export/deletion is implemented and does not authorize production deletions. No privacy request, export or deletion was executed.

1. Verify the requester controls the exact Firebase UID using a recent authenticated session (and recent reauthentication before irreversible identity removal). Do not identify an account solely by a supplied email address, display name or listing ID. Record the verified UID, request scope, operator and approval in an access-controlled request log outside source control.
2. For an export, prepare an encrypted private export scoped to that UID: Auth account fields without password hashes/tokens, public profile and business map, own listings/history, favourites/saved searches/preferences, both follow-edge directions, authored public posts/replies/reviews and participation in conversations. Review shared messages, reports, evidence, moderation notes and other people's private data separately; never export whole collections or raw Admin snapshots indiscriminately. Deliver through an authenticated/approved private channel; no public bucket tokens or public download URL. Agree export expiry and remove the temporary copy according to the approved policy.
3. For deletion, first inventory references and identify approved retention/legal-hold requirements with the operator. Stop participation (remove/disable tester membership for private testing; broader production suspension/Auth controls need coordination). Do not delete Auth or the profile first. Decide the treatment of retained sold/expired adverts and original stable URLs; redact identifying free text/photos only through the trusted lifecycle workflow. Preserve other participants' rights and conversation privacy. Do not silently rewrite transaction history.
4. Plan explicit handling of authored reviews/forum posts, private evidence/reports, media/download capabilities, favourites/searches/preferences, inbound/outbound follow edges and role claims. Revocation of existing token URLs requires media token rotation/deletion; changing Rules alone is insufficient. Bucket soft-deleted bytes can remain recoverable/billable for seven days. Retention durations and legal holds are not automatically defined by that bucket setting.
5. Review the concrete dry-run inventory and retention/redaction plan; obtain explicit approval for mutations. Use a scoped, reviewed trusted operator tool with retry/audit tracking, never the emulator cleanup script. Verify no foreign records were deleted. Disable/revoke the identity and remove obsolete profile/private data only at the planned step; verify fresh sessions and revoked tokens.
6. Close the request with a record of actions, exceptions, retained categories and any remaining expiry tasks. Do not promise complete deletion while recoverable media, retained chat/history or a hold remains.

For initial localhost tests, use the two designated real accounts, genuine non-sensitive content and a small bounded session. Do not upload identity documents, third-party PDFs or confidential evidence until the evidence inspection/retention workflow is complete. Stop and handle an actual export/deletion request through the operator before continuing; the current UI correctly states these self-service features are not implemented.

Before public launch, approve retention durations, request identity/reauthentication policy, access-controlled request intake, operator ownership/response process, shared-record redaction rules, export delivery/expiry and a tested resumable deletion implementation. The absence of that implementation remains a public-launch blocker; this checklist is not an automated privacy service.
